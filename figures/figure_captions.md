# Current manuscript figure captions

Aligned to the 21 October 2026 fivefold manuscript.

Figure 1. Conceptual workflow and hydraulic parameterization of Manning-based SWOT discharge estimation. (a) SWOT provides water surface elevation (WSE), width, and slope, while gauge discharge supports local calibration. The field photograph illustrates discharge measurement (Tim Merrick/USGS). (b) Observation screening, width correction, and effective channel geometry. Red crosses denote observations excluded by quality control; red dashed segments with open circles indicate anomalous widths requiring adjustment. Green segments show usable widths after screening and correction. Brown shading represents the effective base area A₀, parameterized by WSE₀ and width₀. Blue shading represents the unscaled area increment obtained by integrating corrected width above the minimum calibration WSE, hmin. The coefficient β scales this increment, and n controls hydraulic resistance in the discharge equation. Cross-sections are schematic. (c) QC-retained observations are assigned to five folds within five WSE strata. Each fold is validated using a model calibrated on the other four, and each observation is validated once. Validation observations are classified as below, within, or above their gauge-specific calibration WSE range. Width relationships and hydraulic parameters remain fixed during validation.

Figure 2. Data screening and use. (a) Locations of 542 calibration gauges, colored by the number of QC-retained overpasses. Basin boundaries provide geographical context. (b) Unique gauge-overpass counts at successive data-preparation stages, including the seven-record requirement for fivefold CV. The fivefold subset contains 7,615 observations at 422 gauges.

Figure 3. WSE-based width correction. (a) Gauge-level Pearson r between original SWOT WSE and daily gauge water level Z at 422 CV gauges, using 7,615 same-day pairs. The quoted spread is IQR/2 across gauges. (b) Calibration anchors and corrected curve at gauge 30700300 in fold 3. (c–d) NSE and RMSE for four configurations combining observed or corrected widths with fixed or jointly calibrated hydraulic parameters. Fixed controls use reference n, β = 1, and the geometry rule in Section 2.7. All configurations use identical validation observations. Blue circles include all WSE states and orange triangles select within-range observations. Points and whiskers show medians and Q25–Q75 across five fold scores. NSE uses a symmetric-log axis and RMSE a logarithmic axis. Light points show individual fold scores.

Figure 4. WSE-stratified fivefold CV and hydraulic calibration. (a–b) NSE and RMSE calculated from the combined validation pairs separately in each fold. Every observation is validated once. (c–d) Component comparisons on identical validation observations, contrasting the full model with calibrated n and fixed geometry, fixed reference n, or β = 1. Blue circles include all WSE states, and orange triangles indicate observations within their own calibration WSE range. Points in (c–d) are medians across five folds and whiskers are Q25–Q75. The NSE axes use a symmetric-log scale and the RMSE axes a logarithmic scale. The fixed-geometry alternative still uses local discharge to calibrate n. Light points show individual fold scores.

Figure 5. Hydraulic extrapolation and extreme residuals. (a) NSE calculated across gauges within, above, and below each observation’s calibration WSE range. Counts are median pairs per fold. Points and whiskers give the median and Q25–Q75 across five fold scores. (b) Fraction of total squared error contributed by the largest approximately 1% of absolute full-model residuals in each fold. (c) Gauge-level factor error against distance beyond the nearest boundary divided by calibration WSE span. Each gauge contributes its median out-of-fold factor error in the bin. Points and whiskers summarize these gauge medians, displayed on a logarithmic axis. Arrows indicate Q75 above the displayed range. (d) NSE calculated across gauges before and after exclusion of the largest residuals, using the original fits and the same foldwise exclusions for within-range observations. Blue circles include all WSE states and orange triangles select within-range observations. Screening uses measured validation discharge and is retrospective. Light points show individual fold scores. Numbers in (c) give contributing gauges.

Figure 6. Paired comparison with four unconstrained SoS Version 3 products. (a) The 108 gauges contributing 794 unique out-of-fold overpasses to the common four-product sample. Gray points show other calibration gauges. The five folds contain 174, 150, 157, 158, and 155 identical pairs for all five estimates. (b–c) NSE and RMSE calculated across gauges, with medians and Q25–Q75 across five validation folds. Local calibration uses additional gauge discharge, so the comparison represents the combined benefit of the implementation and ground constraints on this common sample. It has different coverage from the broader evaluation in Table 1. Light points show individual fold scores.

Figure 7. Gauge-level performance and spatial variability. (a) NSE calculated once from the combined out-of-fold predictions at 418 eligible gauges, with all 542 calibration locations shown. Gray points lack an eligible score. (b) Distribution of gauge NSE on a symmetric-log axis. The quoted spread is IQR/2 across gauges. (c) Paired local-minus-product NSE differences calculated on the same unique observations at each gauge. Points and whiskers show the median and Q25–Q75 across eligible gauges for each product. Positive differences favor the local model. (d) Spatial distribution of paired differences for SIC4DVar. Symbol size in (a,d) increases with the number of evaluated observations. Products use different paired gauge samples.
