# Figure captions for manuscript version 15

The captions below are reproduced verbatim from the clean v15 manuscript. Figure numbering follows the actual captions, including the reordered Figures 5 and 6.

## Figure 1

Figure 1. Workflow for locally calibrated SWOT discharge estimation. (a) SWOT water surface elevation (WSE), width, and surface slope are matched with available gauge discharge at each station. (b) Calibration WSE and width observations define W(h), and gauge discharge constrains effective geometry, the area-increment coefficient β, and Manning resistance n. The resulting A(h), W(h), and slope enter the modified Manning equation. (c) WSE-stratified validation assigns calibration and validation observations across five water-level strata. Forward temporal validation fits earlier records and evaluates later blocks, expanding the calibration record between blocks. Filled blue and open orange marks represent calibration and validation observations. Validation WSE is classified as below, within, or above the calibration range. Parameters and W(h) remain fixed during each validation block. Both evaluations are retrospective because satellite QC uses the complete record. The river scene, cross section, and validation diagrams are conceptual illustrations.

## Figure 2

Figure 2. Data support and the contribution of local calibration. (a) Numbers of retained observations at the 542 full-record calibration stations, with hydrological boundaries for geographic context. (b) Numbers of station-overpasses at successive stages of data preparation; the horizontal axis is logarithmic. (c) Six principal model comparisons using identical held-out observations within each WSE assignment. Paired NSE differences are first summarized across assignments at each station and then by the median across stations. Points show this median, and bars show 95% intervals from 2,000 whole-station bootstrap resamples. Positive values favor the full model. The empirical WSE–Q baseline is highlighted, and Table S1 reports all ten comparisons. The fixed-geometry n-only alternative is a simplified local comparator. (d) Nested WSE-balanced calibration subsets of increasing size are evaluated against the same holdout from the first assignment. Results use 56 common stations, 55 of which have valid NSE; the shaded band shows the across-station interquartile range.

## Figure 3

Figure 3. Discharge estimation under WSE-stratified and forward temporal validation. (a) Station NSE for WSE-stratified observations within the calibration range, summarized across assignments. Each eligible assignment requires at least five pairs and nonzero variation in both observed and estimated discharge. NSE values below −1 share the lowest color class. (b) Each plotted estimate is the median of repeated predictions from assignments in which that observation fell within the calibration WSE range. The lower strip includes estimates below 0.01 m³ s⁻¹, and R² and NSE are calculated on the original discharge scale. (c) Distributions of station NSE for within-range WSE tests, all WSE tests, and all temporal tests; each group includes its own eligible station sample. (d) Within-range pooled NSE and all-state median station NSE for each of the ten WSE assignments. The series use different validation subsets and aggregation levels and illustrate sensitivity to the calibration–validation assignment.

## Figure 4

Figure 4. Error within and beyond the calibration WSE boundaries. (a–b) Pooled NSE and MdAPE for validation observations below, within, and above the calibration range. WSE statistics are medians of the ten per-assignment pooled summaries, and temporal statistics combine later test blocks. (c–d) Factor error versus distance beyond the nearest calibration boundary. Distance is divided by the positive calibration WSE span and separated into bins with boundaries at 0.25, 0.5, and 1. Discharge factor error is max(Qestimated/Qobserved, Qobserved/Qestimated). For the station-balanced summaries, absolute log ratios are first summarized within each station-assignment and then within each station. The across-station median is exponentiated to express the result as a multiplicative factor. Bars show 95% intervals from 2,000 whole-station bootstrap resamples, and labels give station counts. An arrow indicates an interval extending beyond the plotted scale; the upper limit is 9.53 × 10¹⁶ for the nine-station WSE below-range >1 bin. Predictions are evaluated with their original fitted parameters. Station and event samples and calibration WSE spans can vary among distance bins.

## Figure 5

Figure 5. Extreme-error concentration, residual-screening sensitivity, and width-input effects. All panels use 6,152 distinct WSE validation observations at 270 baseline-evaluable stations, with each observation represented by its median prediction across assignments. (a) Cumulative contribution to squared error as observations are ordered by hydraulic-model absolute error, largest first; the horizontal axis is logarithmic. (b–c) Pooled NSE and RMSE before screening, after excluding errors exceeding a factor of ten, and after excluding the 62 observations with the largest absolute errors. Both models use the same retained observations, selected by hydraulic-model residuals, and retain their original fits; labels are rounded. (d) Proportion of errors exceeding a factor of ten for observed validation widths, fitted validation widths, and the empirical WSE–Q curve. WSE, slope, calibration width anchors, and fitted parameters are identical for the two hydraulic variants. Screening uses observed discharge and is a retrospective diagnostic. These pooled summaries use a different aggregation from the per-assignment statistics in Table 1.

## Figure 6

Figure 6. Matched comparisons with existing unconstrained SoS V3 discharge products. (a) Stations eligible for a paired MdAPE comparison with at least one product after high-confidence network mapping; open orange squares indicate temporal stations. (b) Product-specific numbers of stations used for MdAPE. (c–d) Paired differences in MdAPE and NSE, summarized by the median across eligible stations, with 95% station-bootstrap intervals. Each comparison uses identical positive observations and requires at least four pairs per station. Positive differences indicate lower MdAPE or higher NSE for the local model. Both panels use symmetric-log axes. Eligible samples differ among products and can also differ by metric; the fixed four-product pooled cohort is reported separately in the text. The local model uses gauge discharge during calibration, so the comparison reflects both the local implementation and the additional gauge constraints.

## Figure S1

Figure S1. Examples of calibration width constraints. Four stations from full-record reconstruction were selected because they had the largest numbers of anchors affected by the width-growth cap. Gray points show calibration anchors, dashed orange lines show the isotonic fit after the initial-width check, and solid blue lines show the growth-capped relation. WSE is shown relative to the lowest anchor at each station for readability. These examples illustrate how the constraints alter the curves at selected stations; Table S1 reports their paired performance effects across eligible stations.

## Figure S2

Figure S2. Effective parameters and numerical diagnostics for full-record reconstruction at 542 stations. (a–c) Fitted n and β and their joint distribution, colored by objective loss. (d) Percentages of fits within 1% of the transformed search interval from each parameter bound. (e) Distributions across stations of coefficients of variation (CVs) calculated from three optimization starts. Boxes show the interquartile range (IQR), and whiskers extend to 1.5 IQR. Outliers are omitted from this panel, and only positive CVs are shown on the logarithmic axis. (f) Sensitivity of reconstruction NSE to a multiplier applied to fitted n, with geometry and all other inputs held fixed. When the multiplier is ×1.2, the n bounds clip the perturbation at 65.1% of stations. These diagnostics assess stability and sensitivity within the numerical search; identifying physical parameters requires additional hydraulic observations.
